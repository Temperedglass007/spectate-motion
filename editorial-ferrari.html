Ferrari, In Their Own Words

What 2026 feels like inside Maranello — through the words of Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton, from the car's changing character to the pressure of turning pace into results.

Editorial desk · 25 Aug 2026Long readAnalysis

Primary-source-led This editorial is built from Formula 1's published interviews and Ferrari driver comments. The writing and interpretation are original SPECTATE work; short quotations are retained only where they sharpen the driver's own voice.

Two drivers, one Ferrari — different readings

There is a useful tension in the way Leclerc and Hamilton describe Ferrari. Hamilton has repeatedly spoken about the gains that can arrive when a setup change suddenly makes the car more intuitive. In Zandvoort qualifying, he called the difference from Ferrari's changes “unbelievable” and said he was much happier with the car. Leclerc, on the same weekend, was more cautious, pointing to changing engine modes and tyre preparation as reasons the car behaved differently from session to session.

“The difference it makes in the car is unbelievable.”
Lewis Hamilton · Formula 1, Dutch GP qualifying

That contrast is revealing without being contradictory. Hamilton was describing a setup breakthrough from his side of the garage; Leclerc was describing the instability he still felt in the qualifying process. One was talking about what Ferrari had unlocked. The other was talking about what Ferrari still needed to understand.

Leclerc: the pace was there, but the weekend was not clean

After Zandvoort, Leclerc's assessment was more measured than the final result suggested. Ferrari finished fourth and fifth, but he believed the underlying race pace was strong. He also pointed directly at qualifying: starting position had limited what was possible on a circuit where overtaking is difficult. The late Virtual Safety Car then made the recovery even harder.

“Our pace was strong, and I believed in it until the very end.”
Charles Leclerc · Formula 1, Dutch GP race-day comments

His comments also carried a forward-looking note. Monza is different: Ferrari's home crowd, a different track profile and a weekend where the team will want to convert whatever it learned at Zandvoort. Leclerc described Monza as a special place for the team and said he was excited to return.

Hamilton: ambition is not the problem

Hamilton's Zandvoort frustration came from the same competitive instinct that has defined his Ferrari season: he wants the car and the team to maximise every opportunity. During the race he criticised the delay while running behind Leclerc, then explained afterwards that he had given everything and that he was there to win.

“I want to win.”
Lewis Hamilton · Formula 1, Dutch GP

That is not a complicated statement, but it is a useful lens for Ferrari's current position. A fourth and fifth can be evidence of competitiveness while still feeling like a missed opportunity. Hamilton's frustration was not proof that Ferrari was falling apart; it was evidence that the margin between a strong weekend and a podium is now narrow enough to hurt.

What the two voices tell us

Leclerc's language is about precision: understand the qualifying deficit, understand the tyre preparation, understand why the car changes between sessions. Hamilton's language is about conversion: when the car is there, use it, and do not give away time. Put together, those perspectives describe Ferrari's 2026 challenge better than either one alone.

The Scuderia does not appear short of pace everywhere. The harder question is whether it can make that pace repeatable across qualifying, strategy and race execution. That is where the next phase of the season becomes interesting.

And then comes Monza

Ferrari heads to its home race with both drivers carrying different kinds of evidence. Leclerc has a clear desire to clean up qualifying and maximise the starting position. Hamilton has shown that, when the setup clicks, he can feel a major difference in the SF-26. Neither is describing a finished product.

That is why the most interesting Ferrari story right now is not a rumour, a contract headline or a single result. It is the conversation between two elite drivers trying to extract more from the same machine — and telling us, in their own words, where they think the remaining seconds are hiding.
