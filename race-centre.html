Team strategy watch

Race-day tactical outlook

Alpine

Gasly / defend

Baseline: protect clean air, cover Russell's undercut and preserve enough tyre life to respond if the race goes long.

Mercedes

Russell / attack

Mercedes can pressure P1 with an early undercut, longer first stint or pit-window flexibility. Antonelli's rear-grid start adds a second strategic variable.

Ferrari

Leclerc + Hamilton

Two cars inside the top five give Ferrari options to split strategy, cover McLaren and exploit home-race track position.

McLaren / Red Bull / recovery

The other strategic battles.

McLaren: Piastri starts P3 and can attack the front while Lando Norris looks to recover from the team's Friday pace deficit. Red Bull: watch long-run pace, straight-line efficiency and whether Verstappen can become an alternate-strategy threat. Antonelli: starting at the rear makes tyre choice, clean air and Safety Car timing especially important. Midfield: Monza's long straights can create DRS trains where one good stop can unlock several positions.

Things to look out for

Six race-defining moments.

01 / Turn 1 launch
476m from pole to the first braking point. Gasly must survive the opening attack from Russell and Piastri.

02 / Undercut window
One stop is the leading baseline. Pit timing matters because Monza's pit-lane loss is roughly 24 seconds.

03 / Rear tyre temperature
Traction out of the chicanes and rear-axle overheating can decide the second half of a stint.

04 / DRS trains
Top speed and battery deployment can turn a car behind into a genuine threat, especially after a pit stop.

05 / Safety Car / VSC
Neutralisation can turn the one-stop calculation upside down. F1's pre-race data puts historical Monza Safety Car probability around 50% and VSC around 38%.

06 / Antonelli charge
The championship leader's recovery from the back is one of the race's biggest stories — and a potential strategic disruptor.
