A rulebook reset

F1, redesigned
for 2026.

Lighter cars, active aerodynamics, a different energy story and the end of traditional DRS: the essential changes shaping this new era.

Long readUpdated for Dutch GP weekendOfficial sources linked

The 2026 Formula 1 rules change the relationship between power, aerodynamics and energy management. The cars are smaller and lighter, active aero replaces the old DRS concept, and electrical power becomes a much larger part of the power unit.

01 / More electric influence.

The 2026 power unit shifts the balance toward electrical deployment. The MGU-H is removed and MGU-K output rises to 350 kW, while the cars use 100% advanced sustainable fuel.

Recharge

Energy can be recovered through braking and other mapped strategies. Drivers and teams manage battery state as part of the lap.

Boost

A driver-operated deployment tool can apply saved electrical energy to attack or defend, subject to the technical rules.

02 / Two wing states, not one DRS flap.

Active aerodynamics adjust front and rear wing angles between Corner Mode and Straight Mode. The system is not simply an old DRS flap with a new name.

Overtake Mode

A proximity-based aid can make extra electrical energy available when a driver meets the relevant detection condition.

Important distinction

Active Aero describes the wing-state system. Overtake Mode describes the proximity-triggered energy advantage. They are related, but not identical.

03 / Smaller. Lighter. Less drag.

Formula 1's official explainers describe a shorter wheelbase, narrower overall dimensions and a meaningful reduction in weight compared with the previous generation. The prior ground-effect tunnel philosophy is replaced by a flatter floor concept.

04 / An 11th team changes the maths.

Cadillac expands the grid to 22 cars. The qualifying format adjusts with six drivers eliminated in Q1 and Q2, while Q3 remains a 10-car contest.

The short version

Power gets more electric. Aero gets more active. The cars get smaller.

That is the 2026 reset. For definitive technical wording, always use the FIA regulations rather than this editorial summary.
