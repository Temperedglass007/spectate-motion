Race-day strategy desk

Analysis / not a live team radio feed

Alpine

Protect Gasly.

Keep track position from P1, cover the Mercedes undercut threat and avoid giving Russell a clean DRS run after the stops.

Mercedes

Attack P1.

Russell has the strongest immediate pressure point. Expect Mercedes to use tyre life, the pit-window and a possible undercut to turn P2 into the lead.

Ferrari

Split the calls.

Leclerc P4 and Hamilton P5 give Ferrari two strategic cards: protect track position with one car while using the other to react to rivals.

Teams to watch

McLaren · Red Bull · Mercedes · Ferrari · Alpine

McLaren: Piastri starts P3; watch whether race pace can recover the qualifying deficit. Red Bull: long-run pace and top-speed efficiency could make Verstappen a late factor. Mercedes: Russell is the immediate pole threat, while Antonelli starts at the rear after the engine penalty. Ferrari: Leclerc/Hamilton have home-race track position but need to avoid strategy compromise. Alpine: Gasly must turn a surprise pole into clean air and a controlled first stint.

Things to look out for

Sunday's pressure points.

01 / Turn 1
Gasly has only 476m from pole to the first major braking zone. Russell and Piastri can attack immediately.

02 / The first pit window
Monza's pit-lane loss makes a one-stop the leading strategy. Timing the stop against traffic and the undercut is critical.

03 / Tyre management
C3 hard, C4 medium and C5 soft are available. Rear-axle overheating and traction out of the chicanes are major concerns.

04 / DRS trains
Track position matters, but a car with strong top speed can remain dangerous behind another car through the long straights.

05 / Safety Car / VSC
Monza has historically produced meaningful neutralisation risk. A Safety Car or VSC can completely rewrite a one-stop race.

06 / Antonelli recovery
Starting at the back turns the championship leader into the race's biggest recovery-watch driver.
