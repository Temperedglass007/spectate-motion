The probability desk / Round 13

Who wins
at Monza?

The Dutch GP is now filed: Lando Norris won from pole, with Kimi Antonelli second and George Russell third. Friday at Monza has now supplied a new signal: Leclerc led FP1, Russell led FP2, Antonelli was third in FP2, and Ferrari showed strong one-lap potential. The desk has refreshed its view without treating practice times as qualifying results.

Italian GP win view

Editorial probability snapshot before Monza qualifying. These are estimates, not bookmaker odds or a live model.

Charles Leclerc

27%

Ferrari's home race, Monza's long straights and Leclerc's qualifying ceiling make him the leading single-driver pick in this snapshot.

Lando Norris

18%

Back-to-back wins in Hungary and the Dutch GP strengthen the form signal. McLaren's race pace keeps Norris near the top, though Monza's characteristics are different from Zandvoort.

Kimi Antonelli

19%

Antonelli arrives as championship leader after finishing second at Zandvoort. Mercedes' overall 2026 strength keeps him firmly in the win conversation, while the known Monza grid-penalty issue is a material downside.

George Russell

23%

Russell won the Dutch GP Sprint and finished third in the Grand Prix. His Mercedes pace keeps him in the front group for a low-drag circuit.

Oscar Piastri

6%

A McLaren win remains plausible, but the current evidence gives Norris the stronger single-driver case entering Monza.

Max Verstappen

4%

Red Bull remains capable of a step forward, but Verstappen's Zandvoort retirement means the desk keeps a conservative pre-Monza confidence level.

Rest of field

3%

Hamilton, Ferrari's second car and the rest of the field retain an outside path through qualifying, strategy, weather and reliability.

What changed

The desk refreshed — without inventing a signal.

Norris won the Dutch GP, Antonelli finished second and Russell third. The Italian GP is next on 4–6 September. Until qualifying provides a new competitive signal, the probabilities are held rather than artificially adjusted.
